# ROADMAP.md — the source of all work

**This file is not optional.** Every feature the agent builds flows down
from here. If it isn't on this roadmap, it doesn't get built; if it needs
building, it gets added here first. One item ships per weekly cycle
(see `WEEKLY.md`).

`provisional: true` — the queue below was seeded at setup by the agent.
The human re-orders it.

## North star

EE Workcenter is the environment an agent steps into to take a KiCad
project from schematic to fabrication outputs with every gate green,
without a human running a tool by hand. Everything here serves that:
the toolchain installs itself, the gates are the definition of done, and
the skills tell the agent which gate comes next.

The owner's measure, 2026-11-10: a small model reaches the same working
design as a large one, and no electrical engineer has to find afterwards
what the gates left out. So what an engineer would ask lives in a gate
that runs it (corners, the frequency plan), and what comes next lives in a
command that says it (`workcenter next`), never in the reader's judgement.

### 1. A schematic held to its parts' ratings
- **Promise:** A command reads a project's schematic, the facts of each part and a rule pack. It fails on each fault a reviewing engineer names at a schematic review: a pin above its rated voltage, a capacitor or a resistor past its derating, a rail loaded past its source, and a support part a datasheet asks for that is absent or out of range. Each failure prints the rule, its source document with the clause or the page, and the part. What the command cannot judge it lists as not judged.
- **Evidence:** 5 fixture boards with 1 planted fault each fail, each on its own rule: a 5 V output on a 3.3 V pin, a rail loaded past its regulator, an IC with no capacitor on its supply pin, a buck converter whose inductor saturates below its peak current, and a capacitor that keeps under half its value at its working voltage. The fixture without a fault passes. The command's run on each of the 6 example projects is in `STATUS.md`.
- **Use case:** Review a schematic before layout (a new use case).
- **Scope guard:** The command judges and derives no fact. A rating comes from a facts file that names its datasheet. A limit comes from a rule pack that names its document. No limit is written in the tool's code.
- **Status:** in progress (week of 2026-11-16). On 2026-11-17 the command, 2 packs, the 6 fixtures and the `ratings` skill landed. 1 number stands in the code: the 0.9 efficiency that TI SLVA477C names, taken when a switching regulator's facts state none, and the report says so. The 6 examples have no facts, so their runs judge nothing. The same day both packs were read a second time, `workcenter next` got its ratings step, and the command ran on a real board, a scratch copy of Last Hit v0.16 with facts from its own library's datasheets: 140 lines hold, 4 are not judged and 6 fail, each of the 6 a real stress (`STATUS.md`); what that run showed wrong in the command is in the changelog. The attack of P14 step 6 ran the same day: a reviewer who did not write the command returned 30 findings on its verdicts and on the files it takes, and each is fixed with a test or named in the skill as a reading coarser than the circuit. A second reviewer made 431 single changes of the engine and wrote 158 tests for the 205 that passed, 157 of which are in the repository; 4 findings on the engine are fixed, and a shunt with a stated current now holds its 2 sides together. Left: a second reading of that board's facts; a fuse's rule in the 2 packs; a net stated as following another net, so that 2 nets 1 source moves together (a cell's 2 ends) are not subtracted at their extremes; parts that conduct 1 way or in 1 state (a level shifter's transistor, a zener that clamps its node); the needs on 1 pair of nets counted in 1 unit; a current line for a choke or a diode in a row with a load; and a mutation list for the engine, as P18 keeps one for firmware.

### 3. Circuit blocks written from the makers' notes

## Later — candidates, rated

Each row is 1 thing to build: the gap that a project met, where it was
met, and 6 scores (`PROCESSES.md` P35 step 7). How often it was met (f),
what it cost (c), what building it turns into a command or a refusal (l)
and how many projects it serves (g) make its value; the effort (e) and the
risk (r) of building it make its price. The rating is f + c + l + g less
e + r. The rows stand in order of rating, the highest first:
`python3 tools/harvest.py later` holds each row to that, and a test runs
the same check in `make verify`. "Checked" is the day a reader who did not
write the row tried to refute it; `no` says none has yet, and such a row's
scores are its writer's alone. The queue's next item comes from the top
unless the owner names another, and a candidate that ships leaves the
table for "Shipped".

## Explicitly not doing

- Reverse engineering KiCad with REA or similar — KiCad is open source with an official IPC API and CLI. REA belongs to workcenters built on closed tools.
- GUI or screen automation of KiCad — every operation goes through `kicad-cli` or the IPC API.
- Bundling a patched KiCad — the workcenter installs the upstream PPA build or uses the upstream image.
- Installing `kicad-packages3d` by default — 3.3 GB for 3D models no gate reads.

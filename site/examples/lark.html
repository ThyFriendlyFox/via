Lark

A ceiling-fan bulb you talk to

Lark is an A19 light bulb for a ceiling fan's light kit. It screws into the fan's E26 socket, hangs base-up, and takes 120 V mains like any bulb. Its light is 16 LEDs in 2 strings, warm (2700 K) and cool (5000 K), each driven with a steady current, so the light shows no 120 Hz ripple and no PWM: under a fan's turning blades a ripple shows as a strobe. It is built to be talked to: 2 microphones at the dome's tip face the room, and an ESP32-S3 with 8 MB of PSRAM runs Espressif's ESP-SR wake word and command models inside the bulb, with no hub, no app and no network. A 3-place slider on the neck sets what else it joins: Alexa (Zigbee 3.0, to an Echo with a Zigbee hub in it), nothing (voice only), or Apple Home (HomeKit over Thread, to a HomePod or an Apple TV). An ESP32-C6 module runs that radio and the light.

The hard parts

What is hard about it, and where each part lives

A bulb's inside is a cone, a sphere and a 20 mm tube, and a mains supply, 2 LED drivers, 2 processors and 2 microphones must fit in it

4 boards in 2 directions: 2 stand at 90 degrees to each other, 2 lie across them, joined by soldered tongues through slots. bulb.py holds the housing as numbers and every part's height, and stops a design whose part leaves the housing or meets another board

Every node of a non-isolated bulb sits at mains potential

IPC-2221B spacings in rules/lark.kicad_dru: 0.8 mm between the mains nets and the logic on the outer layers, 0.2 mm inside the board; the housing is the insulation

A fan's blades turn any light ripple into a strobe

average-current bucks with analog dimming (no PWM at the LEDs), and 9.9 uF of bulk that holds the bus over the strings' highest voltage through every 120 Hz valley: 117.0 V at the worst corner against the 103.3 V the controllers need

A switch on the side of a mains-powered bulb

the slider is a magnet outside the sealed housing over 2 Hall switches inside it: no contact, no hole, no insulation to bridge

Voice in the bulb, with no cloud

an ESP32-S3 with 8 MB of PSRAM for ESP-SR's front end, wake word and command models; its own radio is not wired, and it reaches the network only through the C6's UART, which carries commands, never sound

The microphones in a hot, closed dome

the ear at the dome's tip, the coolest place in a bulb that hangs base-up; SPH0641LU4H-1, rated to 100 C at 3.6 V

How it was built

Every step of the design, with what it changed

Each step as the README's "What changed" sections (v0.1/README.md) records it, oldest first.

  1. v0.1

    The first bulb

    The owner asked for a bulb for a ceiling fan that takes mains power, joins Alexa or Apple's home with a switch on its side, and takes voice in the bulb itself as the main way to work it, because their Matter devices did not work well. Then: make it as compact as it can be to fit the bulb, and grow it, or stack boards in more than 1 direction, where it cannot fit. ../LOOP.md has each pass.

What is open

What is still to be done on this board

  • Standby power: about 0.9 W from the mains with the voice front end running, over Title 20's 0.2 W. A wake-word chip that listens at a few mW, waking the S3, is the candidate.
  • The microphones' spacing: 10.4 mm, against the 4 to 6.5 cm ESP-SR's 2-microphone front end is tuned for. Its beamforming gains little at 10.4 mm; a single-microphone front end, or the 2 microphones across the dome's width, is the choice to make.
  • A wake word of the bulb's own: ESP-SR ships "Hi ESP", "Computer" and others; "Alexa" is one, and would wake the owner's Echo too. Espressif trains a custom word as a service.
  • A parts catalog with each line's distributor number (parts.csv), then workcenter sources per board and workcenter costs for 1 bulb and 10; and the verification package (P28).
  • The fan's own control: many fan light kits dim with a triac or switch the light from a remote's receiver. v0.1 expects a steady 120 V at the socket; a phase-cut input is not handled.
  • 230 V mains: the bus, the LinkSwitch's ratings and the spacings are for 120 V rms.
  • The firmware: ESP-SR on the S3, the UART protocol, Zigbee and HomeKit on the C6.
  • The housing: the neck's ribs that hold the driver, the disc's ledge, the gasket rings at the microphones' holes, and the slider's track, drawn from bulb.py's numbers.
